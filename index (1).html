What this is
Skin color isn't random and it definitely isn't a racial category. It follows a geographic gradient shaped almost entirely by UV radiation levels at different latitudes. The further you are from the equator, the less UV you get, and ancestral skin tones got lighter to match. This map shows that pattern.

The hypothesis
The Vitamin D/Folate Hypothesis (Jablonski & Chaplin, 2000) explains it as a biological tradeoff. Near the equator, darker skin protects folate from UV breakdown. Away from it, lighter skin allows Vitamin D synthesis from weaker sunlight.
The takeaway
Skin color is a cline, a smooth continuous gradient, not a set of boxes or racial groups. Natural selection shaped it over thousands of years of human migration. It tells us about sunlight, not about who people are.
ⓘ  Map colors show UV intensity by latitude, which shaped ancestral pigmentation over thousands of years. Modern countries are highly diverse due to migration and don't reflect a single skin tone.

01 / 03
Folate
Protection
Near the equator UV is intense year-round, and it breaks down folate in the bloodstream. Folate is a B vitamin that's critical for fetal development, so low levels are linked to failed pregnancies and neural tube defects. Over thousands of generations, populations living near the equator evolved higher melanin concentrations to shield folate from UV degradation.
High UV · Dark pigmentation
02 / 03
Vitamin D
Synthesis
Further from the equator UV levels drop, especially during winter. The body needs UVB radiation to produce Vitamin D, which is essential for bone density, calcium absorption, and immune function. Lighter skin evolved to compensate for weaker sunlight by letting more UVB through to the deeper layers where Vitamin D is made.
Low UV · Light pigmentation
03 / 03
A Cline,
Not Categories
The gradient on this map is continuous. There are no clean breaks between skin tone groups. This is exactly what you'd expect from a trait shaped by natural selection across a gradient environment. Skin color varies the way height varies, gradually and across a spectrum. It is not a valid basis for racial classification.
